Reproducibility Materials

Model-Assisted Calibration for Integrating
Probability-Based Survey Samples

Yanhao Lu & Lingxiao Wang  ·  Department of Statistics, University of Virginia

Under review R GitHub repository ↗

Overview

When a scientifically important variable is expensive to measure, it is often observed only in a smaller internal survey. Larger external surveys frequently collect overlapping but incomplete covariate information. Borrowing information from external sources can substantially improve the efficiency of regression analyses based on the internal survey alone, without requiring a correctly specified outcome model.

We develop model-assisted calibration estimators for this two-survey integration setting. The estimators adjust the internal-sample weights so that the reweighted influence functions match external auxiliary information. A Taylor linearization variance decomposition accounts for the complex stratified multistage cluster designs of both surveys.

Methods

All estimators solve the internal-sample weighted estimating equations under GREG-adjusted weights. Design consistency holds without assuming a correctly specified outcome model.

Setting 1 — pooled microdata

Individual-level data from both surveys are combined. Calibration adjusts s₁ weights so that the s₁ weighted estimating equations match their pooled-sample counterparts, reducing variance from subsetting.

Setting 2 — external summaries

When external microdata are unavailable (e.g., due to data-sharing restrictions), point estimation uses only the fitted coefficient estimates from the external analysis; variance estimation additionally requires their variance–covariance matrix.

Variance decomposition

The analytical variance of the calibrated estimator decomposes into four components: V11 (outcome-model variance), V22 (calibration-variable variance), V12 (cross-terms), and the external-survey contribution — each estimated by Taylor linearization using the survey package’s svytotal variance estimator applied to influence-function totals.

Data Application

We estimate the association between DXA-measured total body fat and all-cause 10-year mortality using NHANES and NHIS public-use data.

Role Survey Sample size Key feature
Internal NHANES 2003–2004 n₁ = 1,543 DXA body fat (expensive covariate X₂)
External NHANES 2001–2002 & 2005–2006 (combined) n₂ = 3,107 BMI, activity, smoking, alcohol; no DXA
External NHIS 2003–2004 n₂ = 24,086 BMI, activity, smoking, alcohol; no DXA

Outcome: all-cause death within 10 years of interview (NCHS 2019 mortality linkage). Analysis restricted to adults aged 40–69 with complete covariate and DXA records.

Reproducing Results

Simulation study — fast route (seconds)

Reads pre-saved canonical Monte Carlo summaries. No simulation reruns needed.

setwd("path/to/simulation")
